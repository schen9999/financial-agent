## Audited (Exec Summary + Outlook)

### Executive Summary
Caribou Biosciences is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing allogeneic CAR-T cell therapies, operating with a market capitalization of $168.3 million, accumulated losses of $596.5 million, and a cash runway of at least 12 months supported by $142.8 million on hand. The stock has declined 55% from its 52-week high of $3.54 to $1.57, reflecting deepening investor skepticism about the company's path to profitability and its need for substantial additional capital to advance its pipeline beyond current phase 1 trials. The single most important near-term variable is the clinical readout from vispa-cel and CB-011, as trial data will either validate the therapeutic approach and unlock financing options or further erode investor confidence in the platform.

### Outlook
The directional outlook for Caribou Biosciences is **cautious**, weighted by the company's acute dependence on external financing, the absence of approved products, and a cash position that provides only a limited operational window. The primary tailwind is the scientific promise of allogeneic CAR-T cell therapy as a potentially transformative modality in oncology — a category that continues to attract partnership and licensing interest from larger biopharmaceutical players. The central headwinds are the funding gap that stands between the company and a pivotal trial for vispa-cel, the unproven nature of the underlying technology at commercial scale, and a market that has already repriced the stock sharply downward. Investors should monitor three key variables: the clinical data emerging from phase 1 trials for vispa-cel and CB-011, which will serve as the primary proof-of-concept signal; the company's ability to secure a partnership, licensing deal, or non-dilutive financing arrangement that extends runway beyond the current 12-month window; and any regulatory guidance that clarifies the pathway to a pivotal trial. A materially positive clinical readout combined with a credible financing solution would be the conditions most likely to shift this view toward constructive; conversely, disappointing trial data, a dilutive capital raise at distressed terms, or further delays to the pivotal trial timeline would deepen the cautious stance.
