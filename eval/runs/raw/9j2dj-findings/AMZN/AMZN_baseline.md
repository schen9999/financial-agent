## Audited (Exec Summary + Outlook)

### Executive Summary
Amazon.com, Inc. is a global technology and commerce conglomerate commanding dominant positions across e-commerce, cloud computing, and digital advertising, with annual revenue of $775.7 billion, a net income of $135.3 billion, and a market capitalization of $2.79 trillion. The stock is notable now because Amazon is simultaneously monetizing its scale through high-margin AWS and advertising growth while deploying capital into social commerce partnerships and energy infrastructure—a combination that has driven a net profit margin of 17.4% and keeps valuation, at 20.8x trailing earnings, in a range that remains debatable relative to peers. The single most important near-term variable is whether AWS margin expansion continues to accelerate or faces compression from rising energy and infrastructure costs, as that dynamic will largely determine whether the forward P/E of 24.9x proves justified.

### Outlook
The directional lean on Amazon is **cautiously constructive**, grounded in the company's demonstrated ability to expand margins across diversified, high-quality revenue streams while continuing to invest in durable growth vectors. The primary tailwinds to watch are AWS services-margin trajectory, the pace of advertising revenue maturation, and the degree to which social commerce initiatives like the YouTube partnership translate into measurable marketplace gains. On the headwind side, investors should monitor energy infrastructure costs and the regulatory scrutiny surrounding gas-powered data center development, which could slow AWS capacity expansion or increase its cost profile—directly pressuring the segment that anchors the bull case. International exposure adds a layer of complexity: currency volatility, tariff escalation, and geopolitical friction could weigh on what is already a lower-margin segment of the business. The thesis would strengthen if AWS margin expansion proves resilient despite rising infrastructure investment, if social commerce partnerships demonstrate clear revenue contribution, and if renewable energy commitments successfully insulate operating costs over time. Conversely, the view would turn more cautious if regulatory headwinds materially constrain data center buildout, if competitive intensity in cloud accelerates pricing pressure, or if new market expansion initiatives continue to consume capital without a visible path to profitability.
