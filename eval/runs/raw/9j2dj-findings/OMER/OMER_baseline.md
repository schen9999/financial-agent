### Executive Summary
Omeros Corporation is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company whose financial performance is now entirely dependent on YARTEMLEA, its first and only FDA-approved product, which received approval in December 2025 and generated $38.4 million in revenue against a market capitalization of $1.37 billion. The stock is notable now because it has staged a dramatic recovery from a 52-week low of $3.94 to $18.95, reflecting a surge in investor optimism around YARTEMLEA's commercial launch, yet the company's $116.1 million in annual operating cash burn against $171.8 million in cash reserves underscores how narrow the margin for execution error truly is. The single most important near-term variable is the pace and breadth of YARTEMLEA's commercial adoption — specifically whether physician uptake, payer reimbursement decisions, and revenue generation accelerate quickly enough to outrun the company's cash consumption before additional capital is required.

### Outlook
The directional outlook for Omeros is **cautiously constructive but highly contingent**, with the investment thesis hinging almost entirely on YARTEMLEA's commercial trajectory in what is still an early and unproven launch phase. The primary tailwind is the product's recent FDA approval and the evident market enthusiasm that has driven the stock's sharp recovery, suggesting investors are pricing in meaningful commercial potential. However, the headwinds are substantial and immediate: the company's operating cash burn relative to its cash reserves leaves limited room for a slow ramp, reimbursement coverage gaps and payer pushback could suppress net realized pricing, and the company's limited commercial infrastructure introduces meaningful execution risk. Investors should monitor YARTEMLEA prescription volume trends and payer coverage decisions as the most direct leading indicators of commercial success, alongside quarterly cash consumption as a gauge of financial runway. The refinancing of the convertible senior notes due June 2029 is a secondary variable worth tracking as capital market conditions evolve. The thesis would strengthen materially if YARTEMLEA demonstrates accelerating physician adoption, broad and timely reimbursement coverage, and a visible path toward reducing cash burn; conversely, the thesis would weaken — and the speculative risk profile would intensify — if launch momentum stalls, payer access proves restrictive, or the company is forced to raise capital on dilutive terms.
