## Audited (Exec Summary + Outlook)

### Executive Summary
Stitch Fix is a subscription-based online personal styling service competing in the broader apparel retail sector, generating $1.33 billion in revenue while carrying a market capitalization of $407 million at a current share price of $3.05. The stock is notable now precisely because it sits near the bottom of its 52-week range ($2.95–$5.94), trades with a negative forward P/E ratio, and remains in an acknowledged turnaround phase with no clear near-term catalysts for recovery. The single most important variable shaping the outcome is whether the company can stabilize and grow its active client base, as continued declines in that metric directly undermine revenue sustainability and any path to profitability.

### Outlook
The directional lean on Stitch Fix is **cautious**. The company faces compounding headwinds — a multi-year decline in active clients, a China-concentrated supply chain exposed to tariff and trade policy risk, and a marketing model that has not demonstrated reliable returns on incremental spend — with no clearly identified near-term catalyst to reverse these trends. Investors should monitor active client trajectory above all else, as stabilization or growth there would be the earliest credible signal that the turnaround is gaining traction. Equally important to watch are supply chain diversification efforts and China exposure, given the potential for tariff escalation to further compress already thin margins, and marketing efficiency trends, where improving client acquisition cost relative to client lifetime value would indicate the business model is strengthening. On the regulatory front, the evolution of privacy rules and cookie-based tracking restrictions warrants attention given the company's reliance on data-driven personalization as a core competitive differentiator. The view would become more constructive if the company demonstrates consecutive periods of active client stabilization, a credible path to operating profitability, and reduced supply chain concentration risk — conversely, further client attrition, margin deterioration, or an adverse shift in the macroeconomic environment for discretionary consumer spending would deepen the cautious stance.
