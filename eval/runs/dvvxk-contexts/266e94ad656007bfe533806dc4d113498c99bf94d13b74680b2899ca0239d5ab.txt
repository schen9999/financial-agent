The company discloses several primary risk factors related to its CRISPR genome editing technology business:

## Clinical Development and Technology Risks
- CRISPR genome editing technology has only recently been clinically validated for human therapeutic use, and in vivo CRISPR-based technologies remain relatively new with largely unproven therapeutic utility
- The company's approaches to developing therapies using CRISPR systems are unproven and may never lead to marketable products
- No genome editing in vivo therapy has been approved in the U.S., EU, or other key jurisdictions, making regulatory approval uncertain
- Successful development requires solving numerous technical challenges, including safe delivery of therapeutic agents to target cells and optimizing efficacy and specificity

## Regulatory and Approval Challenges
- Clinical development is lengthy, expensive, and uncertain in outcome
- Regulatory authorities have very limited or no experience with CRISPR-based therapeutics, particularly in vivo therapeutics, which may require additional testing and delay development
- Regulatory agencies may impose stricter requirements or require more extensive preclinical and clinical testing compared to traditional therapies
- Clinical trials may fail to demonstrate safety or efficacy, or produce negative or inconclusive results

## Market Adoption and Public Perception Risks
- Public perception and media coverage of potential safety or efficacy issues may adversely influence participation in clinical trials and physician/patient acceptance
- Ethical concerns related to genome editing and CRISPR-based therapies may impact adoption
- Physicians and healthcare providers are often slow to adopt new products and may view these therapies as too complex or risky without appropriate training
- Healthcare providers and payors may determine that benefits do not outweigh costs

## Operational Challenges
- Manufacturing and supply chain challenges for preclinical, clinical, and commercial supplies
- Inability to establish clinical endpoints that regulatory authorities consider clinically meaningful
- Challenges in patient enrollment and retention in clinical trials
