## Clinical Development and Regulatory Risks
- CRISPR genome editing technology has only recently been clinically validated for human therapeutic use, and in vivo CRISPR-based therapies remain largely unproven
- No genome editing in vivo therapy has been approved in the U.S., EU, or other key jurisdictions
- Clinical development is lengthy, expensive, and uncertain, with no guarantee that programs will prove effective and safe in humans
- Regulatory authorities have very limited or no experience with clinical development of CRISPR-based therapeutics, which may require additional testing and delay development

## Technical and Manufacturing Challenges
- Successfully developing safe delivery methods to target cells and optimizing efficacy and specificity remain unsolved issues
- Challenges in sourcing preclinical, clinical, and commercial supplies for materials used to manufacture product candidates
- Establishing manufacturing capabilities and access to sufficient commercial manufacturing capacity

## Market Adoption and Perception Risks
- Public perception and media coverage of potential safety or efficacy issues may adversely influence participation in clinical trials and physician/patient acceptance
- Ethical concerns related to genome editing and CRISPR-based therapies may impact adoption
- Physicians and healthcare providers are often slow to adopt new products and may view these therapies as too complex or risky without appropriate training
- Healthcare providers and payors may determine that benefits do not outweigh costs

## Operational Risks
- Inability to establish clinical endpoints that regulatory authorities consider clinically meaningful
- Challenges in patient enrollment and retention in clinical trials
- Potential suspension or termination of clinical trials due to safety concerns or regulatory requirements
